Underglaze privacy policy

Last updated 27 October 2026

In short: Underglaze has no accounts and no servers of its own. Your progress and settings stay on your phone. If you haven't bought "Unlock everything", the game shows one advert a day through Google AdMob, and Google collects some information to show and measure it. In the UK, the EEA and Switzerland, you're asked first. If you buy the unlock, Google Play handles the payment and the app only learns that you own it.

Who we are

Underglaze is a tile puzzle game for Android, made by Laurence Timms, trading as Whimwham Games, in the United Kingdom. For anything about this policy or your data, email chuquet@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

The game keeps these on your phone, in the app's own storage:

  • the floors you've played and solved, with your turn and swap counts;
  • boards you're part way through;
  • your settings, such as worn tiles, haptics and the six extras;
  • whether you've done the practice floor, the date the last advert was shown, and whether you own the unlock.

None of this is sent to us or to anyone else. We don't have a copy and can't see it. It's deleted when you uninstall the app or clear its storage in Android's settings.

Sharing a floor

When you tap Share, the game makes a picture and a short line of text about the floor you solved, and hands them to Android's share menu. Where they go next is up to you and the app you choose. The game sends nothing until you tap Share, and it never includes anything else about you.

Advertising (Google AdMob)

If you haven't bought "Unlock everything", the game shows one full-screen advert a day, after your first solve. Adverts come from Google AdMob, run by Google. To show and measure them, and to prevent fraud, Google's software in the app collects information such as:

  • your device's advertising ID;
  • your IP address and the approximate location it suggests;
  • details of your device and the app, such as the model, the Android version and the language;
  • how you interact with adverts, such as whether one was shown or tapped.

Google uses this as described in how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services and in Google's privacy policy. We don't receive this information about you as an individual. AdMob only gives us totals, such as how many adverts were shown.

Your choices about adverts

  • In the UK, the EEA and Switzerland, the game asks for your consent through Google's consent message before any advert is requested. You can accept or decline personalised adverts. If you decline, Google may still show adverts that aren't personalised, which use less information.
  • Change your mind at any time: in the game, go to Settings, then "Privacy and ad choices", then Review.
  • On your phone: you can reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's settings, under Privacy and then Ads, or Google and then Ads, depending on your phone.
  • No adverts at all: buying "Unlock everything" removes adverts permanently.

Buying "Unlock everything" (Google Play)

The one purchase in the game is made through Google Play's billing system. Google handles the payment under Google's privacy policy and the Google Play terms. We never see your card or payment details. The app asks Google Play whether you own the unlock, and keeps a note of the answer on your phone so the unlock still works offline. Google Play's own records let you restore it on a new phone.

What we don't do

  • We don't ask for your name, email address, contacts, photos or location.
  • We don't use analytics or crash-reporting services.
  • We don't sell your information or share it with anyone, apart from the Google services described above.

Children

Underglaze isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them. The game itself collects no personal information from anyone. If you think a child's information has been collected through the adverts, contact us and we'll help you ask Google to remove it.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you have rights over your personal information, including to see it, correct it, delete it, and object to its use. Because the game keeps nothing about you itself, most requests are best made to Google, which holds the advertising and purchase information. You can manage it at your Google account. We're happy to help: email us at the address above. If you're unhappy with how your information has been handled, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.

Changes to this policy

If the game starts to collect anything new, this page will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
